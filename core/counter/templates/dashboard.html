1 · Choose a servo (board channel 0–15)

Tips
  • Each servo keeps its own pulse for every number, so different servos can be set differently.
  • While the servos are on (after Start), typing a pulse or pressing − / + moves the servo straight there. Test moves it to the pulse in the box again.
  • Grey pulses are suggestions for numbers you haven't set. Nothing is saved until you press Save.
  • Go gently near the ends of travel, and stop if the mechanism hits its limit.
  • Saving turns the servos off. Press Start again for the next servo.
  • Settings → Servos shows every servo's saved pulses at a glance.

Servo board

Checking

Checking the servo board…

Give the servos their own power.

The Pi's 5 V pin is only for a quick test with a servo or two. For more, power V+ from a separate 5 V supply and connect its − to GND.

Wiring guide
Pi pinServo board
1 · 3.3 VVCC · board power
3 · GPIO2SDA · data
5 · GPIO3SCL · clock
7 · GPIO4OE · Stop switch
9 · GNDGND · ground
2 · 5 VV+ · servo power (quick test only)
1 · 3.3 V → 1 kΩ resistorOE · keeps servos off while the Pi starts

Keep VCC at 3.3 V; 5 V goes to V+, never to VCC. Leave OE connected to pin 7, not to ground.

Each servo · three wires

Servo wireIts channel on the board
Signal · yellow / orange / whitePWM / signal
Power · redV+ / positive
Ground · brown / blackGND / negative

The leftmost digit's servo plugs into channel 0, the next into channel 1, and so on up to channel 15. Wire colours vary, so check the labels.

Moving one servo at a time does not guarantee enough current: a servo can still stall or brown out the Pi. With a separate supply, remove the pin 2 → V+ wire and never join the supply's + to the Pi's 5 V. Stop turns the servo signals off; it does not cut servo power.

Servos
Saved pulse for each number, in microseconds (µs)

      

        
Each servo stores its own pulse for every number 0–9. Servo numbers are the board's channels, 0–15. To change a pulse, choose Calibrate on that servo's row. Allowed range 600–2400 µs; your servo may need less.

        

          
ServoNow0123456789Calibrate

          
        

        
“Now” is the number and pulse Counter last sent; servos don't report where they actually are. After a restart it is unknown until the display moves again, and without position feedback a move from an unknown position can't be guaranteed to be a single step.

        

          
          
          
        

        
Only one servo receives a PWM signal at a time, and its signal stops after each move, so the mechanism must hold its own position.
