Pulse widths are specific to your servo and mechanism. Suggested values are unsaved starting points. Test gently; stop before a mechanical end stop.

A full-turn digit wheel needs a position-controlled servo with sufficient travel. Continuous-rotation servos cannot select a digit without position feedback.

One digit module per servo, using channels 0 through 15. Only the selected channels move.

Keep VCC at 3.3 V. The 5 V motor wire goes to V+, never to VCC. OE stays connected to GPIO4; do not tie it to ground. USB-C powers the Pi.

Repeat all three wires for each display. Leftmost display → CH 0, next → CH 1, through CH 15. Check connector labels; wire colours can vary.

Pi-powered prototype: power budget unverified.

The diagram shows Pi pin 2 → V+ for your setup. Moving one servo at a time does not guarantee enough current: a servo can still stall or brown out the Pi.

For a suitably rated external servo supply, remove the pin 2 → V+ wire, connect supply + to V+ and supply − to shared GND. Never connect external supply + to the Pi’s 5 V rail.

Use a 1 kΩ OE pull-up to override the board’s weak pull-down; verify OE is high during boot. Stop outputs disables signals; it does not disconnect servo power.
